Sight Reader: options, reading and help

Four surfaces replace the one long Options dialog. The toolbar gains Read, which holds what is read from this piece (clefs and parts). Options keeps only the preferences, as three short cards. Help becomes real help, with the clef reference as one of its tabs.

1. The toolbar

Read sits beside Options. Its tooltip, and a quiet summary after it when something is left out, says what is being read, so a reader never has to open it to find out. Help moves to the right end beside the piece title.

2. Options: three short cards, no paragraphs

The PUDL settings panel, a column of cards. Choices from a short list are segmented controls, which show every choice at once and take one click, where a select hides them. On and off settings are switches. The explanations that were under every control move to Help, and a short title attribute stays on each control for a hover.

3. Read: what to read from this piece

A per-piece choice, so it has its own small dialog and its own button, and it can grow with the score. Clefs are toggle chips (a score with one clef shows none, as now). Parts are a scrolling list with a filter, which appears when there are more than eight, All and None, and "Only" on a row to hear one part alone, as a mixing desk's solo does. The count is a quiet summary, not a statistic about notes. A part with nothing to read is greyed with a reason.

4. Help: how to use Sight Reader, with the clefs as a tab

The Help button opens real help in three tabs (PUDL's tabs): first steps, answering, and the clefs. The clef staves from today's Help are the third tab unchanged. A separate Clefs button is the alternative if you would rather have the reference one click away.

Help

Sight Reader shows a score and puts a cursor on the first note. Name that note, and the cursor moves on. A wrong answer flashes and the cursor stays; after a few wrong tries the answer is shown.

  1. Choose a score with Scores…, or drop a MusicXML file on the page.
  2. Answer on the keyboard on the screen, with the letters A to G on your keyboard, or on a guitar or violin fingerboard (Options).
  3. Choose what to read with Read…: a clef, or some of the parts of a score.
  4. Practise a passage by giving a first and last bar, and Loop to go round again.

Scores are MusicXML files, compressed (.mxl) or not. Notation programs such as MuseScore export them.

Tabs two and three: Answering (the keys A to G, # or = for a sharp and - for a flat before the letter, intervals and chords, the piano and the fingerboards, typed letters and clicked keys) and Clefs (the four staves already drawn).
